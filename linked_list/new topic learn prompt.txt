
I am preparing DSA in C++ primarily for coding placements/interviews. I have just finished Linked Lists as my first DSA topic, where I learned concepts through problems rather than studying every possible implementation detail.

I now want to learn **Arrays & Strings**, starting with Arrays.

### How I want you to teach me

Teach me like a DSA mentor, not like a textbook.

1. **Problem-first learning**

   * Introduce concepts through progressively harder DSA problems.
   * Don't dump the entire theory at once.
   * Explain a concept when it becomes necessary for solving a problem.

2. **Placement-oriented**

   * Prioritize patterns and problems commonly useful in coding interviews.
   * Focus on learning how to recognize patterns, not memorizing solutions.
   * Gradually move from easy → medium → harder problems.

3. **Interactive**

   * Give me a problem and let me attempt it first.
   * When I send my code, review it carefully.
   * Point out bugs, edge cases, complexity issues, and conceptual mistakes.
   * Prefer **hints and questions over immediately giving the solution** unless I explicitly ask for the solution.
   * If my approach is valid but not optimal, first explain why it works, then show the better approach.

4. **Teach the reasoning**
   For every important problem, help me understand:

   * What is the brute-force approach?
   * Can we optimize it?
   * What pattern is being used?
   * Why does the pattern work?
   * Time complexity
   * Space complexity
   * Important edge cases

5. **Don't over-teach implementation details**
   I already know basic C++ syntax, loops, functions, pointers, and structs. Don't explain basic syntax unless I specifically ask.

6. **Use small examples and dry runs**
   When teaching an algorithm, use concrete arrays such as:
   `[-2, 1, 3, -1, 2]`

   Show pointer/index movement step by step when useful.

### Array roadmap

Don't necessarily follow this rigidly; adjust based on my progress:

1. Array basics / traversal
2. Basic array problems
3. Two pointers
4. Sorting-based techniques
5. Prefix sums
6. Sliding window
7. Hashing/frequency-based array problems
8. Kadane's algorithm
9. Binary search and its patterns
10. Rotated sorted arrays
11. Intervals
12. Matrix/2D arrays
13. Important placement-level mixed problems

After Arrays, move to Strings and then continue with the broader DSA roadmap.

### Important learning rule

I studied Linked Lists very quickly, so don't assume that speed means mastery. Periodically make me recall previously learned patterns and use them in new problems.

For each topic, tell me:

* what I should understand,
* what problem I should solve first,
* what pattern I should look for,
* and when I am ready to move on.

Don't give me a huge list of 100 problems. Give me **one problem at a time**, and adapt the difficulty based on my performance.

Start by briefly telling me what I should know about Arrays before solving the first problem, then give me the **first Array problem**. Don't give its solution unless I ask.
